Foods

About Foods

Meat, poultry, game, and seafood proteins organized by biological group and cut, each with its own wine pairing logic based on fat, texture, and cooking method.

Categories

  • Animal Protein Foods
  • Fungi Protein Foods
  • Plant Protein Foods

Explore Foods

  • Beef — Beef cuts from rib, loin, chuck, round, and plate span the full range from tender, quick-cooking steaks to long-braised roasts, each with its own wine pairing logic based on marbling and cooking method.
  • Cephalopods — Cephalopods — squid, octopus, and cuttlefish — range from tender when quickly seared to chewy if overcooked, served as tubes, tentacles, and prepared calamari.
  • Crustaceans — Crustaceans — lobster, crab, shrimp, prawn, and crawfish — bring sweet, briny richness that favors bright, high-acid whites.
  • Cured Meat — Cured meats like salami and prosciutto develop concentrated, salty, savory depth through salting, drying, and aging.
  • Fin Fish — Fin fish spans delicate white fillets to rich, oily species, with fat content and cooking method driving which wine style suits each fish best.
  • Grains & Wheat Protein — Grain-based proteins include wheat gluten forms like seitan alongside protein-rich whole grains such as quinoa, amaranth, buckwheat, farro, oats, and barley.
  • Lamb — Lamb cuts from rack and loin to shoulder, shank, and ground carry a distinctive, slightly gamey richness classic in European cooking and pairing.
  • Legumes — Legumes bring plant-based protein to the plate — chickpeas, lentils, and beans with an earthy character that pairs well beyond the expected reds.
  • Mollusks — Mollusks cover bivalves and gastropods — oysters, mussels, clams, scallops, abalone, and conch — prized for their briny, mineral-forward flavor.
  • Mushrooms — Twelve globally recognized edible mushrooms from Agaricus to chanterelle, prized for their savory, umami-rich depth.
  • Mycoprotein — Mycoprotein is a fermentation-derived meat alternative sold as fillets, pieces, grounds, and steaks, valued for a texture closer to meat than most plant proteins.
  • Nuts & Seeds — Tree nuts and oilseeds bring concentrated fat and protein to the plate, from almonds to pine nuts, each with its own roasted, buttery, or earthy character.
  • Pork — Pork spans fresh cuts like belly, loin, and shoulder alongside cured forms, offering everything from delicate roasts to rich, smoky charcuterie.
  • Poultry — Poultry spans chicken, turkey, duck, goose, and quail, with flavor and richness varying widely between lean white meat and darker, fattier cuts.
  • Soy Foods — Soy foods span fresh and fermented forms — soybeans, tofu, tempeh, natto, textured vegetable protein, and miso — each with a distinct texture and depth of flavor.
  • Veal — Veal's delicate, mild flavor across cutlets, chops, loin, and shank makes it a classic match for lighter, more nuanced European wine pairings.
  • Wild Game — Wild game includes venison, elk, bison, rabbit, hare, and wild boar — leaner and more intensely flavored than farmed meat, pairing best with bold, structured reds.
