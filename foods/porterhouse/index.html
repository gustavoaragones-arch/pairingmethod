Porterhouse Steak

About This Entry

Porterhouse is a large T-bone cut from the rear short loin with a generous tenderloin section and a well-marbled strip. Its size and dual texture support full-bodied reds and longer cellar-worthy bottles for special-occasion roasts.

Beginner Guide

Porterhouse looks like a T-bone but must include a tenderloin filet at least 1.25 inches across (USDA definition). It is typically thicker and meant for sharing.

FAQ

What is Porterhouse Steak?
Porterhouse looks like a T-bone but must include a tenderloin filet at least 1.
How should Porterhouse Steak be cooked for best results?
Rich cuts tolerate medium to medium-rare; render fat slowly on roasts and braises. Rest before serving so juices redistribute.
