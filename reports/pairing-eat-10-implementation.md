# PAIRING-EAT-10 — Wine Fault Evidence & Claim-Matched Citations — Implementation Report

## 1. Executive Summary

Implemented PAIRING-EAT-09's strongest P1 finding: a narrowly-scoped, claim-matched external-reference layer for wine-fault pages. Exactly 2 of the 30 published fault pages (cork-taint, Brettanomyces) received exactly 1 independently-verified external reference each, attached to one specific scientific claim on each page. Both sources (The Australian Wine Research Institute) were directly opened and read, not assumed from search snippets; both were confirmed to genuinely support the specific claim they're attached to, with honest caveats recorded where a source only partially covers a claim's detail. No citation implies external validation of Pairing Method's own model. Local verification: **18/18 PASS**. Final result: **LOCAL PASS — DIRECTOR REVIEW REQUIRED**.

## 2. Phase Objective

Implement EAT-09's P1 recommendation: attach a small number of high-quality, claim-matched external references to the wine-fault pages carrying the most specific, quantifiable scientific claims on the site — without creating a bibliography, sitewide citation feature, or any implication that an external source validates Pairing Method's proprietary pairing model.

## 3. Existing Fault Architecture

Wine-fault pages are generated by a shared pipeline: `data/wine-fault-catalog.json` (30 entities) → `lib/taxonomy-wine-fault.js` (page-context builder) → `lib/taxonomy-wine-fault-render.js` (HTML section renderer) → `scripts/generate-wine-faults.js` (writes directly to `faults/<slug>/index.html`, no dist/staging step, unlike the food-tail domains). Each fault entity has `cause`, `how_it_occurs`, and `prevention` free-text fields, each rendered as its own `<section>`.

**Existing evidence terminology, confirmed and respected:** `data/relationship-evidence.json` holds 110 *internal* reasoning-support annotations (documented in `docs/WINE_ONTOLOGY_V2.md` at 2.56% coverage) — confirmed, per EAT-09, to be self-referential (why the model asserts a relationship), never an external source. This phase does not use or modify that file, and does not name its own new data structure "evidence" — it is named `data/wine-fault-external-references.json`, with the field `claims_supported` and record type explicitly documented as distinct from the internal evidence-annotation system.

## 6. Candidate Claims

Per the ticket's required minimum, both EAT-09-named candidates were implemented. One additional candidate was evaluated to confirm the same standard is applied consistently, not just to the two pre-named pages:

- **hydrogen-sulfide** (`"Yeast reducing sulfur-containing amino acids under nutrient deficiency..."`) — a real, accessible AWRI source (Yeast Assimilable Nitrogen page) was found and directly opened. On verification, it confirmed the nitrogen-deficiency/H₂S correlation but did **not** explicitly detail the sulfur-containing-amino-acid mechanism named in the site's claim. Per the claim-matching standard ("if a source only indirectly supports the claim, do not cite it"), **this candidate was rejected, not cited.**
- **volatile-acidity** — not researched in depth in this pass; documented as a legitimate scope boundary (see `reports/pairing-eat-10-claim-source-matrix.json`, `candidates_evaluated_but_not_cited`), not a gap requiring correction. The ticket explicitly instructs not to force every fault page.

## 7. Source Research Method

For each candidate: (1) WebSearch for an authoritative source on the specific claim; (2) WebFetch to directly open and read the actual page content (not rely on search-result snippets); (3) explicit comparison of the source's stated content against the exact site claim text; (4) rejection if support was only partial/indirect (see hydrogen-sulfide above). Preferred-hierarchy consideration: The Australian Wine Research Institute (AWRI) is Australia's dedicated wine-science research institute — squarely a "recognized scientific institution" / "established professional oenology organization" per the ticket's hierarchy, and both selected pages are freely accessible with no paywall (confirmed during fetch).
